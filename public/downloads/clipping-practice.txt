CLIPPING GUIDE: PRACTICE SCRIPT AND ANSWER KEY
Updated October 7, 2026
Guide: https://clippingguide.com/guides/what-is-clipping/#a-worked-clipping-example-what-stays-and-what-goes

This is an original fictional teaching script. It is not a real creator's
transcript or a report of microphone testing. Record your own reading for
practice. The numbers identify lines, not timings in a video.

SOURCE SCRIPT
1. Before we start, I changed the thumbnail and made tea.
2. I bought a new microphone, but my recording still had traffic noise.
3. I thought another microphone would fix it. That was the wrong first step.
4. For the next take, I closed the window and moved closer to the microphone.
5. In this example, that change made the words easier to understand.
6. This does not mean a microphone upgrade is never useful; I checked the setup first.
7. Next time I will talk about lighting.

YOUR TASK
- Write the viewer's takeaway in one sentence.
- Choose a beginning and an ending.
- Identify one cut that would change the speaker's meaning.
- Give your clip an accurate title.
- If you record the script, use timestamps from your actual take in the worksheet.

SUGGESTED EDIT
Remove line 1: it does not explain the problem.
Keep lines 2-3: they establish the problem and the mistaken assumption.
Keep lines 4-5: they explain the change and its result within the example.
Keep line 6: it prevents an unsupported general claim about all equipment upgrades.
Remove line 7: it starts a different topic.

Suggested takeaway: check the recording setup before assuming another purchase
will solve the problem.

Suggested title: A recording setup check before buying another mic.

Meaning check: removing "I thought" and "That was the wrong first step" from
line 3 reverses its point. Preserve the clarification in speech and captions.
